Help

Cards appear in this order, top to bottom. A budget-warning banner can appear above Quick Add; a Live asset prices card can appear between Assets and Liabilities once you've tagged an asset with a ticker/symbol (see Assets below).

Expenses Quick Add

Type a whole expense as free text, e.g. "$14 lunch chipotle debit" - amount, merchant, and a matching account (if you name a payment type like "debit" or a bank name) are detected automatically as you type. This always works with no setup (a built-in keyword parser). If this household's optional Gemma server is configured (GEMMA_ENDPOINT), a second pass runs a moment later and can fill in fields the keyword parser missed or got less precisely - you'll see "Asking Gemma..." then "Parsed by Gemma" in small text under the field. Nothing is saved until you tap Save.

Four fields are required before Save will go through: Amount, Account, Category, Date. Any of the four that's still empty gets a live red border the whole time, not just after a rejected Save - it clears the instant you fill it in. Description/merchant is the only optional field.

If the account it guessed is wrong (or it couldn't guess), just pick the right one from the Account dropdown yourself - it never blocks you, it just doesn't guess when it isn't confident (for example, two Chase accounts and the text only says "chase").

Import CSV (small button below the form) opens a 4-step wizard: pick a file, map its columns (which column is the date and which is the amount are required; description and category are optional, plus a checkbox if the file's amounts are recorded with the opposite sign this app expects), preview every row with likely duplicates flagged, then confirm. Imported rows are marked as history that already happened, so they never touch your account balances a second time (your balance should already reflect them in real life) - use the "Undo this import" button right after if you picked the wrong file or mapping.

Recent History

A combined feed of every expense and every non-expense money movement (a manual balance correction, a liability payment, a transfer between your own accounts, income received, an investment contribution, a logged interest charge, even an account being opened) - not just expenses. Search by merchant/description text, or narrow with the Type/Account/Category/Kind dropdowns and a min/max dollar range - all filters combine (AND, not OR). "Kind" is the one that separates plain expenses from everything else (account activity has no category or payment type of its own, so it drops out of those two filters automatically unless you pick "Activity only").

Tap an expense row to open it for editing or recategorizing. Account-activity rows (anything that isn't a plain expense) can't be edited that way since most of them have no separate edit form - instead every row, expense or activity, has a small undo icon that reverses exactly what it did (restores the old balance, removes the row) and can't be used twice.

Check the box on several expense rows at once to recategorize all of them together in one Apply - your checked selection survives changing the filters above it, so you can search, check a few, search again, and check more before applying.

Budgets

A monthly spending limit per category. Unlike the charts on the Reports page, a budget always applies to the current real-world month - it doesn't follow any month picker. Once you're at 90% of a limit the bar turns amber; over it, red, and a "Budget warning" banner appears at the very top of this page (and a toast fires right at the moment a save actually crosses the line). Set a category's limit again to change it; tap the ✕ to remove it entirely.

Net worth

Assets total minus liabilities total (tracked debts only) - a single number, read-only here. It deliberately does not subtract subscriptions or general spending: a subscription is a recurring future cost, not a debt you owe today, and ordinary spending already reduced an asset's balance directly the moment you logged it. See the Reports page for how this number trends over time.

Accounts

Payment methods only - which card, bank, or wallet you paid with. Accounts are not counted in net worth by themselves; every account automatically gets its own linked Asset (a debit/deposit-style account) or Liability (a credit-style account) behind the scenes, and that linked balance is what actually counts. "+ Add" walks you through picking a type (this decides the account's name, like Checking or Credit Card) and typing the bank or institution name - for a real bank/credit union this is checked against a large built-in list as you type, and if it's genuinely not on the list you're offered an "add it anyway" option rather than being blocked outright.

Tap any account circle to adjust its balance directly: type the new total, or use +/- if you only know the change. A credit-type account instead shows Pay/adjust-owed controls from the Liabilities card below, plus a credit-utilization line and (once you've filled in its statement-cycle details there) a paid-in-full / carrying-a-balance / missed-minimum status. Archiving an account (the small "Archive" link under a circle) removes it from every list and total while keeping its full history intact and fully reversible from "View archived" - unlike the ✕ delete, which is permanent and unassigns its past expenses.

Transfer (button next to "+ Add") moves money between two of your own accounts - Checking to Savings, for example. Only accounts with a real balance behind them can be picked (not a credit card or loan - moving money onto a card is paying it down, which stays on the Liabilities card's own Pay flow below). Shows up in Recent History as a single undoable entry, and reduces the "from" account's balance the moment it's confirmed the same way any other spending does.

Assets

Everything you own that counts toward net worth - not just accounts. "+ Add asset" covers standalone things with no linked account: an Investment, Property, Vehicle, a retirement account you're tracking on its own, or any of roughly fifty specific types (brokerage, 401(k)/Roth/Traditional variants, HSA, crypto, and more). A Vehicle gets an optional depreciation estimate (purchase price, date, and a yearly rate) instead of you updating its value by hand. An investment-flavored asset gets an optional cost basis and allocation bucket, both used on the Investments page; adding a ticker/symbol and quantity here also lets the live-price feature price it automatically, refreshed every 15 minutes for a real stock ticker (see the Investments page's help for the fuller picture, including per-ticker holdings inside an account).

A CD's maturity date shows an upcoming-renewal reminder here once it's within 30 days. Any manually-priced asset (no live feed) that hasn't been touched in 6+ months gets a gentle "this might be stale" notice, just as a reminder to check it's still accurate - nothing changes automatically.

Liabilities

Tracked debt only, split into two groups: Credit liabilities are the automatic ones tied to a credit-type Account above (their balance moves only through real charges/payments, or a manual correction - see below); Other liabilities are standalone ones you add directly here (Loan, Mortgage, or a specific type like a HELOC or student loan) with no linked account.

"Pay" on any liability moves money from a linked asset/account you pick straight to paying it down. The balance can also be corrected directly - set it to an exact figure, or add a specific charge - which is useful when the real statement doesn't quite match what got logged (a fee, a late-arriving charge, a refund); every one of these corrections is written to Recent History as an undoable entry, so nothing changes the amount owed silently.

"Edit details" (small link under any liability) opens interest rate, minimum payment, and due date for any liability, plus type-specific fields: a credit limit (adds a plain utilization line - "$X of $Y used" - once both a limit and balance exist, deliberately with no "aim for under 30%" advice, just the math) for a card/HELOC/line of credit; statement-cycle fields (which day the statement closes, which day payment is due, the last statement balance and date) for a real revolving card, which power a paid-in-full / carrying-a-balance / missed-minimum indicator - paying the statement balance in full by the due date means no interest at all, carrying any of it accrues interest even if every payment was on time, and paying only the minimum avoids a late fee but not the interest. Nothing here is ever applied automatically; you confirm each interest charge yourself from the small "Log interest" link when it's ready. A HELOC additionally gets a draw-period-end date.

Debt payoff strategy

Compares two orders for paying extra toward your debts: avalanche (extra goes to whichever has the highest interest rate) and snowball (extra goes to whichever has the smallest balance). Type an optional monthly amount beyond everyone's own minimum, and both strategies show real months-to-debt-free and total-interest numbers side by side - it never tells you which one to actually use, the same "show the math, not advice" line every calculator in this app holds. A liability is left out of the comparison (and counted in the note below the numbers) if it's missing an interest rate or minimum payment, already at $0, or an active HELOC in its draw period - the same conditions that already hide the per-liability payoff line above.

Profile (top right)

Account security is at the top: your sign-in email (read-only) and a place to set or change your password - this is what actually keeps an installed home-screen icon signed in (a magic-link email always opens in regular Safari, which an installed icon can't reach). Below that, two optional groups: deal-eligibility details (military/student/first-responder status, and so on) used to match you against real subscription discounts on the Subscriptions/Bills page, and financial-context details (housing, employment, household size, goals) used only to give the Reports page's "Ask about your spending" feature something real to reason about - neither group is used anywhere else. "Download all my data" exports a plain JSON file of everything in your own tables.

"Recurring expenses found" only appears when the app spots a bill-like pattern in your expense history. "Found online" only appears once at least one candidate has actually been found - see its own note below. "Income sources" is unrelated to the bills above it - money coming in, not going out - kept on this page since it works the same way.

Monthly total / Yearly

The sum of every active subscription/bill's amount, normalized to a month and a year regardless of each one's own billing cycle (a $120/year plan counts as $10/month here). This is a forecast based on what you've entered, not a count of real charges - a charge only becomes real, dated spending once you use Mark as paid (see below) or it shows up through Quick Add/CSV import like any other expense.

Recurring expenses found

Only shown when your expense history has a pattern that looks like a bill (the same merchant, a similar amount, roughly the same interval). Tap one to start tracking it here as a real subscription/bill instead of just a repeating expense - nothing is created automatically without you tapping it.

Savings found

Compares what you're paying against a small built-in catalog of plan types (student, military, family-plan, and similar eligible pricing) using your Profile's deal-eligibility fields (military/student/first-responder status, and so on) - fill those in on the Log page's Profile modal to get real matches here.

Found online

An optional feature: a background weekly search looks for live subscription pricing and writes candidate findings here, clearly labeled "unverified" with a link to check the source before you trust it - nothing here is ever applied automatically. Hidden entirely until at least one candidate has actually been found. A "pattern match" note with the real matched text means the price was found directly (no AI involved) - shown for exactly the same reason the source link is, so you can judge it for yourself before promoting it.

A small "Last updated" line shows how recently the search last ran. If that run hit a problem (a rate limit, a timeout), a warning line explains what happened instead of leaving you to guess why results look thin or stale - amber for a partial run, red if the whole run failed.

Add / edit a subscription or bill

"+ Add" (top right, or at the bottom of the full list) opens a form: service name, a free-typed category (with suggestions from common ones, but you can type your own), amount, billing cycle (monthly/quarterly/semiannual/annual/other), next renewal date, which account it's paid from, an Active checkbox (inactive ones drop out of the Monthly/Yearly totals and upcoming renewals), an Essential checkbox (a bill you need, versus a discretionary subscription - recorded for your own reference), and notes.

Mark as paid (only shown once the subscription has an account linked and has been saved) logs today's date as a real, dated expense against that account - the exact same effect on your balances as typing it into Quick Add - and automatically rolls the next-renewal date forward by one billing cycle. Use it the day you're actually charged, not when you first set the subscription up.

Upcoming renewals / All subscriptions

Upcoming renewals lists anything due in the next 30 days, for a quick glance - it's read-only here, tap the full list below to actually edit one. All subscriptions/bills is the complete list, active and inactive both; tap any row to open it back in the edit form above.

Income sources

Recurring income (a paycheck, and similar) - genuinely separate from the bills above, kept on this page because it's logged automatically the same way a bill charge is: source, amount, cadence, which account it's deposited into, when the next one's expected, and an Active checkbox. Cadence covers weekly, biweekly, semimonthly (two fixed pay days a month, like the 1st and 15th - not just "every ~15 days," which would slowly drift off your real payday), monthly, annual, and one-time. A one-time source automatically turns itself inactive right after it's logged, so it never logs twice.

Only accounts with a real balance behind them can be picked as the deposit account - not a credit card or loan, the same restriction Transfer (Log page) uses and for the same reason. Each deposit shows up in Recent History as "Income received," undoable like any other entry there.

Cards appear in this order, top to bottom. The month selector (top right) and its "Selected month" / "Subscriptions/Bills" totals apply to everything except the last four cards - see below for which.

Month selector and totals

Everything on this page except the bottom four cards (Account balance history, Cash flow forecast, Net worth trend, and indirectly the Q&A/monthly report below) is scoped to whichever month you pick at the top right. The first two totals under it are that month's full expense total and just its Subscriptions category slice. The third, "Emergency fund," is always current rather than scoped to the selected month - it's your liquid account balances (checking, savings, cash, and similar - not retirement, investment, or loan accounts) divided by your average spending over the last 3 months, shown as a plain number of months covered. No color, no "aim for X months" - just the math, the same way this app never turns a real number into advice.

Ask about your spending

Type a question in plain English (e.g. "How much did I spend on food last month?") and get an answer generated from your own real data - optional, and only works if this household has a Gemma server configured; otherwise it tells you plainly that it isn't set up rather than guessing. It can also draw on your Profile's financial-context fields (housing, employment, goals) if you've filled those in, for a more useful answer to something like "should I pay down debt or save right now?" - it never gives investment advice, only reasons about the numbers you've actually entered.

Questions can also reach further back than the usual recent-months data: a background search looks through your older transactions for whatever specifically relates to your question (e.g. "what did I spend on my trip in 2019") and adds just those to what the AI sees, on top of - not instead of - the regular recent-months data. A line under the answer says how many older transactions were found when this happens. Like the rest of this feature, it's optional and silently does nothing extra if it isn't set up yet.

Monthly report

A written summary generated on a schedule by a separate household server, not something you trigger from here - shows "No monthly reports yet" until that optional service has actually run at least once. The app is fully usable without it.

Monthly Expense Log

Every plain expense in the selected month (not account activity like balance corrections - this list is about spending specifically). Tap one to edit or recategorize it, same as Recent History on the Log page. CSV exports the visible rows to a spreadsheet file; Print / PDF opens a clean, separate printable page you can save as a PDF from your browser's own print dialog.

By category / By account / By payment type

Three bar-chart breakdowns of the selected month's spending, sliced three different ways - which category it went to, which account paid it, and debit vs. credit vs. cash. Each bar's dollar amount is written right on the label so you don't need to hover to read it.

Last 6 months

A bar per month, total spending only, for the six months ending at whichever month is selected above - the one chart on this page that shows more than a single month at once (everything else above it is that one month only).

Income vs. expense

Paired bars, income against expense, for the same six months as the chart above it - income comes from whatever you've logged on the Income sources card (Subscriptions/Bills page), either automatically as each recurring source comes due or from a real deposit you entered yourself. Below the chart, "Savings rate" is (income − expense) ÷ income for the selected month specifically - a real percentage, shown plainly with no color or "aim for X%" framing, the same restraint the Emergency fund stat and the Liabilities card's credit-utilization line already hold. Shows "—" for a month with no income logged yet (dividing by zero income isn't a real 0% rate, so it's left blank rather than shown as one) - a month where you genuinely spent more than you earned still shows a real negative percentage, not hidden.

Account balance history

Pick any account from the dropdown to see its balance over time, reconstructed from its actual expense and activity history - not scoped to the month selector, since a balance's history spans the account's whole lifetime, not one month.

Cash flow forecast

Pick any account to see its balance projected forward 30 days, built only from what's actually known and scheduled: that account's active subscriptions/bills and active income sources. It deliberately does not blend in an average-spending estimate - a forecast built only from real scheduled items is honest about what it knows, rather than looking more predictive than this app can actually claim to be. A bill or paycheck due today isn't shown as a separate point (it's already reflected in the starting balance); one due tomorrow or later shows up as a change in the line on the day it's expected.

Net worth trend

One point per calendar day you actually had the app open (net worth is snapshotted automatically on load, at most once a day) - a day you never opened the app simply has no point, rather than a guessed value filling the gap. Also not scoped to the month selector, for the same whole-lifetime reason as the account history chart above. Needs at least two days of history before a line can be drawn at all.

Cards appear in this order, top to bottom. "Daily recap" and "Market overview" each only appear once there is real data for them - a finished trading day for the first, at least one index/mover/headline for the second.

Daily recap

A once-a-day look back at the session that just finished: the five biggest moves among the same fixed watchlist of 20 large-cap stocks, each with its closing price and percentage change, plus how many of the twenty finished up and where the four index-tracking ETFs ended. Written once per trading day after the close, so it describes a completed day rather than a moving number.

Where a real, recent headline about that company exists, it appears under the symbol with its source, and you can tap through to read it. A headline is what was being reported about that company around that day, not a statement of why the price moved. The app deliberately does not claim to know the cause of a move - it shows you the real number and the real coverage and leaves the connection to you.

A mover with no headline shows nothing rather than something unrelated. That is on purpose: the news source often returns generic market round-ups that merely mention a ticker ("which stocks are making waves today"), and putting one of those under a specific stock's move would imply a link that isn't there. Only a headline that actually names the company is used, and the same article is never shown under two different stocks.

The numbers and headlines on this card need no AI and no live search of any kind - every figure comes from prices already recorded and every headline from news already fetched. That means they can't be affected by a daily usage limit the way the AI-written pieces elsewhere on this page can, so they should be there every trading day. A "Last updated" line shows when the recap was last built, with a warning if that run had a problem.

One short paragraph, clearly labelled "AI-written summary", may appear above the movers. That part is generated - it reads the same numbers and headlines shown below it and writes them up in a sentence or two. It is deliberately the only written element here, and it is an extra rather than the point: if the daily AI limit is used up, or the model writes something that strays into recommending or predicting, the summary is dropped and the rest of the card appears exactly as normal. It never recommends buying or selling and never predicts what a price will do next, the same boundary the whole app holds.

Market overview

Today's level and day-over-day change for a handful of major US indexes (S&P 500, Dow Jones, NASDAQ Composite, Russell 2000) - genuinely separate from your own holdings below, this is broad market context, not anything you own. Hidden entirely until an index has actually been found once; even then, an individual index's own exact level can show "not available yet" until its slower weekly search has landed - see below for the "Live via" line that fills the gap in the meantime.

Below the indexes, "Today's top movers" ranks the biggest day-over-day price swings (up or down) from a fixed watchlist of 20 well-known large-cap stocks, each with a short plain-English explanation of why it moved when one's been found. This is informational only, purely about stocks already on the fixed watchlist - not a scan of the whole market, and never a suggestion of what to buy or sell. It appears independently of the indexes above it, so it's possible to see one without the other depending on what's been found so far.

Two different refresh speeds feed this card, and separate lines tell you apart which is which. Real stock prices (your own tracked tickers below, and the movers list here) come straight from a live market-data API with no search step involved, refreshed every 15 minutes - "Prices as of" reflects that directly, both under the movers list and here under the indexes. While this page is open it goes and gets fresh prices itself, about once a minute, straight from the market-data API rather than waiting on the background job - so a window (or an installed home-screen icon) left open all day keeps showing current numbers without you reloading anything, and prices keep updating even when the machine that runs the background searches is switched off. Today's change is measured against the official previous close that comes back with each price, not against whatever the background job last recorded. Outside regular US market hours that same line reads "Market closed" instead and stops flagging the age as a problem, because the last close genuinely is the latest price there is until the next open. Each index also gets a "Live via [ticker]" line the same way - a closely-tracking, highly liquid ETF (SPY for the S&P 500, DIA for the Dow, QQQ for the NASDAQ-100 specifically rather than the full Composite, IWM for the Russell 2000) priced on that same fast, no-search pipeline, since a live feed for the exact raw index point isn't freely available. The index's own exact level shown above that line, mover explanations, and today's market news below all go through a slower, genuinely web-searched pipeline that only runs weekly instead - the separate "Last updated" line reflects that pipeline specifically. If that weekly run hit a problem (a rate limit, a timeout), a warning line explains what happened instead of leaving you to guess why a price looks stale or missing - amber for a partial run, red if the whole run failed.

A real, clickable recent headline (with its source) can also appear under a tracked stock or mover - genuine news, not an AI summary, refreshed roughly hourly on the same fast pipeline as prices. The separate explanation line above it (when present) is still the slower weekly AI-written "why it moved" read - the two can disagree slightly in timing since one refreshes far more often than the other, and that's expected, not a bug.

The bold line at the top ("X of Y tracked large-caps up today") is a plain count, not an AI's read of the market's mood - it's tallying the same real, already-shown prices below it, so it updates every 15 minutes with no dependency on anything that can run out for the day. Colored green or red only when there's a genuine majority one way or the other, gray when it's roughly split.

Below the movers, "Today's market news" shows a short daily read of general market news coverage - up to 5 real headlines you can tap through to the source, plus an overall sentiment (Bullish/Neutral/Bearish) with a one-line reason grounded in that same coverage. This is a summary of what real news outlets are reporting that day, never a prediction and never a recommendation to buy or sell - the same boundary the rest of this app holds everywhere else. It's derived only from real search results, never invented, and appears independently of the indexes/movers above it.

Daily health check

A short, plain-English compilation of numbers already shown elsewhere on this page - never a made-up score. Up to five lines, each only shown when there's something real to say: today's dollar change (or a note that no live pricing exists yet), total gain or loss since cost basis, the single furthest-off-target allocation bucket (only if you've set targets), a flag if any contribution group is over or close to its annual limit (only if you're tracking one), and how many of your holdings currently have a live price. A small colored dot on each line is green for good, red for something worth a look, gray for "no data yet" - and a compact recent-trend chart sits right below it.

The four stat tiles under it (Total value, Total gain/loss, Today's change, Cost basis) are the same underlying numbers, just as plain totals - Today's change specifically needs at least two days of live price history before it can show anything.

Value over time

One point per calendar day you had the app open, same snapshot approach as the Reports page's net worth trend - needs at least two days of history before a line appears.

Holdings

Every investment-type asset, grouped by account: a plain account (like a 401(k) with no per-ticker detail) shows just its own value, while "+ Add stock" lets you record the actual tickers/coins bought inside any eligible account (Brokerage, IRA, crypto, and similar) - modeled after a real trade ticket: ticker, shares, and price per share, with the total cost shown live as you type. A ticker you type is checked against a large built-in list; a genuine miss offers an "add it anyway" confirmation rather than blocking you.

Two entry modes, a toggle at the top of the form. "Already own this" (the default) is for recording an existing position: type shares and the price you actually paid, and price per share fills in as a courtesy default once a recent price is known for the ticker (edit it if you paid something else - this isn't a live quote). "Buying now" is for a purchase happening right now: it looks up a genuinely real-time price for the ticker, then lets you enter either the number of shares to buy or a dollar amount to invest, computing the other one for you at that live price - a dollar amount can land on a fractional number of shares, the same way a real dollar-based brokerage order can. If no live price is available for a ticker (unrecognized symbol, a lookup problem), the form falls back to the same manual price-per-share entry "Already own this" uses, so it's never a dead end. Either way, once a symbol has a price, shares x price is what actually shows as the holding's value, gain/loss, a day-change figure, and sometimes a short note on why the price moved.

"Paid from (optional)" lets you fund the purchase from a real account (Checking, Savings, Cash, and similar) - if you pick one, the amount is checked against and deducted from that account's actual balance, the same way any other spending is, and shows up in Recent History as an undoable entry. It only ever applies to money actually being added (a brand new holding, or increasing shares/price on one you already have) - editing a holding down never touches a funding account, since there's no way to tell a correction apart from a real partial sale. Leave it on "None" to record a holding without tracking where the money came from.

"Log contribution" under any account records new money you actually put in (as opposed to the account simply growing in value) - this is what the Contribution limits card below is measured against, so it deliberately only counts real deposits, never market gains.

Contribution limits

Only appears if you have at least one account type this app tracks a clean annual IRS limit for (like a 401(k) or an IRA) - shows this year's logged contributions against the base limit, in red once over. These are base figures only (no catch-up contributions, no income phase-outs, no filing status), so treat them as a reference, not a substitute for checking your real numbers.

Target allocation

A calculator, not advice: set a risk label for your own reference (it isn't used in any calculation) and a target percentage for each bucket (Stocks, Bonds, Cash, Crypto, Real Estate, Other) that you've tagged your assets/holdings with. It only ever shows your current percentage against that target and the dollar gap between them - it never tells you what to actually buy or sell, and never will.
